Stocks

US market closed (open 14:30-21:00 UK time, weekdays).

The stocks pot buys sharp short dips in the S&P 500 and Nasdaq index funds (SPY, QQQ) and the biggest US companies, but only while each is in a long-term uptrend. Each trade risks about 1.5% of the pot if the stop is hit, and never more than 10% of the pot goes into one trade. Share prices are in US dollars; the pot counts £1 as $1.

Crypto

The crypto pot uses the same rule as the stocks pot on the biggest coins (Bitcoin, Ether, Solana and others): buy a sharp two-day dip, but only while the coin is above its 200-day average. Coins trade every day and swing about three times as much as big stocks, so stops sit 5–15% below the price paid and trades last at most 10 days. It holds at most two of Bitcoin and Ether, and at most two smaller coins, because coins tend to rise and fall together. Prices are in US dollars; the pot counts £1 as $1.

Lessons from losses

After every losing trade, five reviewers each check one step (scan, research, prediction, risk, execution) and decide whether it was bad luck or a mistake. Some losses are expected even when everything is done right, so a lesson only changes the bot once the same mistake shows up 3 times, and lessons can only make it more careful.

Words you'll see

Price = chance
A prediction-market share pays £1 if the event happens and nothing if not. A price of 80p means the market thinks there's about an 80% chance.
Edge
How much better the bot's estimate is than the price, after fees. An estimate of 84% on an 81p bet is a 3p edge.
Kelly sizing
A formula that sets the bet size from the edge and the pot. The bot uses a quarter of what Kelly suggests, and never more than 10% of the pot, so small edges get small bets.
Stop-loss
A safety exit: sell if the price falls a set amount below what was paid. Big news can make prices jump past it.
Dip in an uptrend
A stock or coin that has risen over the past 200 days but fell sharply over the last two. These often bounce back within days; sometimes they keep falling, which is what the stop is for.
Break-even win rate
How often trades must win just to not lose money. Beating it is the real goal; a high win rate alone isn't enough.
Paper money
Pretend money. Prices are real, but no real money is bought or sold.
